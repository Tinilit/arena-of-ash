# Independent hands, hit reactions and front kick

LMB/J/1 uses the front/left hand; RMB/K/2 uses the rear/right hand. Clicking the playfield starts a single light action. Mouse input is converted through the actual camera, so display density and letterboxing preserve the same world region. UI buttons have their own actions and do not also trigger a playfield attack. Context menus are suppressed only on the arena canvas and restored when leaving. Empty hands cannot start attacks. The selected hand supplies its weapon, pose, grip, stats and contact shape; it never silently borrows the other hand's weapon.

Light actions last 1000 ms, with contact allowed at 45–67%, followed by a separate 280 ms cooldown. The weapon's existing clearance path is retimed to give a longer wind-up. Punches have their own anticipation, extension and return. Heavy actions remain in the animation library but are rejected by combat rules, player controls and AI. Each hand owns an independent 1000 ms action and 280 ms cooldown. Inputs can start both hands in one step or stagger them freely. Each swing has its own ID and one confirmed hit; there is no global hand-attack lock. A body kick remains exclusive with hand attacks. There is no energy/stamina resource.

A valid weapon-blade/body contact changes HP once per action. Geometry contains independent hand blades, fists and boots read from actual joint matrices. The resolver still sweeps both moving shapes at the same instant. Its legacy `blade` event field records the actual striking capsule, including a boot, so contact evidence can be validated uniformly. All contacts from one step are collected before reactions interrupt actions, preserving simultaneous dual hits and trades. Pain/stun interrupts both hand tracks.

A normal hit interrupts the defender's action/hop, starts a 280 ms pain pose and blocks commands for 180 ms. A small recoil moves the body about a few pixels. Pain does not freeze the whole match or teleport the character. Facing and action locks are released through their normal transitions.

F/3 or Front kick performs a 1050 ms kick on the ground or in a jump/hop, active at 36–56%, followed by 350 ms cooldown. The front knee chambers, extends nearly straight without hyperextension, and presents a dorsiflexed sole. The rear foot supports the pelvis on the ground; in flight the other leg remains tucked. The kick contact shape is the actual front boot; distance alone never awards damage or pushback. A hit deals zero HP damage, including against an AI guard, and applies 350 ms stun and an 850 ms balance recovery, with an initial 500 units/second impulse decelerating at 600 units/second². Unobstructed travel is about 210 logical pixels. Walls and actor separation limit it.

The backwards recovery cycle has an 800 ms period at a reference speed of 200 units/second and a long step. Its phase advances with actual displacement. A planted foot therefore stays in world space while the other steps back; the victim does not simply slide in a static pose. Player movement/action requests cannot cancel the forced recovery. Bots currently retain normal movement/guard/light attacks and do not request the new kick or special player hops.

The rear-hand throw preview draws back behind the hip, then swings forward/up, releases at 62% of a 1700 ms clip, and follows through. It always uses the rear shoulder, including when the only equipped weapon is in the front hand. Angles are normalized before shoulder limits so equivalent rotations near ±π do not clamp into the wrong pose. The head stays in the torso plane behind the rear arm throughout the throw. No depth swapping is used. See [throw references](THROW_ANIMATION.md) and the [Godot cutout depth guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/cutout_animation.html#remote-transform2d-node). Camp previews preserve inventory; real arena throws use the overarm clip and permanently consume the selected weapon or belt throwable. See [throwing](THROW_ANIMATION.md).

Backpack contents are the owned IDs minus every equipped ID. It shows all unequipped categories together, eight per page; selecting a slot does not hide other categories. Equipping removes the row, and removing gear from a slot restores it to the backpack. Ownership remains in the save, preventing loss or duplicate instances. A compatible selected slot is honored; other categories choose their appropriate helmet/body/hand/belt destination.

A confirmed kick now has a 20% knockdown chance, replacing the normal stagger with the full grounded hold/get-up sequence. See [knockdown animation](KNOCKDOWN_ANIMATION.md). Physical F is layout independent.

Air kicks preserve normal gravity and movement (including the hop launch impulse), retain the rear-leg tuck and solve only the striking leg toward the kick target. Landing restores ground support through the current kick phase. Damage, pushback and knockdown still require a real boot/body contact; jumping adds no automatic hit or invulnerability.

`ArenaMotion` overlays each hand from its own animator and clock. Torso/head deltas are blended together on the same waist frame, while the hand targets, grip, hit capsule and cooldown remain independent. Completed hands blend back to their idle pose while the other hand continues.

Hit feedback uses one opacity tween per rig. A new contact stops the previous flash, starts from full opacity and explicitly restores alpha to 1 on completion or stop, so simultaneous/staggered hits cannot leave a fighter transparent.
