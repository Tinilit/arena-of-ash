# Independent hands, hit reactions and front kick

LMB/J/1 uses the front/left hand; RMB/K/2 uses the rear/right hand. Clicking the playfield starts a single light action. Mouse input is converted through the actual camera, so display density and letterboxing preserve the same world region. UI buttons have their own actions and do not also trigger a playfield attack. Context menus are suppressed only on the arena canvas and restored when leaving. Empty hands punch. The selected hand supplies its weapon, pose, grip, stats and contact shape; it never silently borrows the other hand's weapon.

Light actions last 1000 ms, with contact allowed at 45–67%, followed by a separate 280 ms cooldown. The weapon's existing clearance path is retimed to give a longer wind-up. Punches have their own anticipation, extension and return. Heavy actions remain in the animation library but are rejected by combat rules, player controls and AI. One actor performs one action at a time, so alternating buttons does not bypass the cooldown. Passive stamina regeneration continues.

A valid weapon-blade/fist/body contact changes HP once per action. Geometry contains independent hand blades, fists and boots read from actual joint matrices. The resolver still sweeps both moving shapes at the same instant. Its legacy `blade` event field records the actual striking capsule, including a fist or boot, so contact evidence can be validated uniformly. All contacts from one step are collected before reactions interrupt actions, preserving simultaneous trades.

A normal hit interrupts the defender's action/hop, starts a 280 ms pain pose and blocks commands for 180 ms. A small recoil moves the body about a few pixels. Stamina keeps recovering. Pain does not freeze the whole match or teleport the character. Facing and action locks are released through their normal transitions.

F/3 or Front kick performs a grounded 1050 ms kick, active at 36–56%, costing 18 stamina and followed by 350 ms cooldown. The front knee chambers, extends nearly straight without hyperextension, and presents a dorsiflexed sole. The rear foot supports the pelvis. The damaging shape is the actual front boot; distance alone never awards damage or pushback. A hit deals body-based damage (halved by an AI guard without removing the push), applies 350 ms stun and an 850 ms balance recovery, with an initial 500 units/second impulse decelerating at 600 units/second². Unobstructed travel is about 210 logical pixels. Walls and actor separation limit it.

The backwards recovery cycle has an 800 ms period at a reference speed of 200 units/second and a long step. Its phase advances with actual displacement. A planted foot therefore stays in world space while the other steps back; the victim does not simply slide in a static pose. Player movement/action requests cannot cancel the forced recovery. Bots currently retain normal movement/guard/light attacks and do not request the new kick or special player hops.

The rear-hand throw preview now coils the hand behind the head, leads the elbow forward, releases at 64% of a 1500 ms clip, and follows through. It always uses the rear shoulder, including when the only equipped weapon is in the front hand. Angles are normalized before shoulder limits so equivalent rotations near ±π do not clamp into the wrong pose. During the coil, the head is temporarily drawn over the rear arm using the identical torso frame, then returns to its normal layer. This is authored occlusion, not a moved shoulder or stretched bone. See [throw references](THROW_ANIMATION.md) and the [Godot cutout depth guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/cutout_animation.html#remote-transform2d-node). Throwing remains a preview without projectiles or item consumption.

Backpack contents are the owned IDs minus every equipped ID. It shows all unequipped categories together, eight per page; selecting a slot does not hide other categories. Equipping removes the row, and removing gear from a slot restores it to the backpack. Ownership remains in the save, preventing loss or duplicate instances. A compatible selected slot is honored; other categories choose their appropriate helmet/body/hand/belt destination.
